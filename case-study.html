Project Overview

Making financial data easier to understand

I redesigned key parts of a financial dashboard to improve transaction visibility, simplify navigation, and help users understand their finances faster during everyday workflows.

The challenge

Understanding financial activity felt unnecessarily difficult

The challenge was to simplify a complex payroll and time-tracking system and turn it into an intuitive daily experience. The focus was on making complex financial data easier to navigate, understand, and interpret, enabling employees to quickly access the information they need and gain a clear understanding of their payroll.

The dashboard was designed to give employees a clear, centralized view of their most important payroll and attendance information. It brings together expense reports, approval statuses, time tracking, and key personal documents in one intuitive interface, reducing complexity and making everyday tasks faster and easier to manage.

The goal was not simply to display information, but to prioritize what employees need most and turn a complex administrative system into a simple, actionable daily experience.

The appearance of the old system

One of the key UX decisions was to create a clear hierarchy between overview and detailed data. Recent expenses and receipts are presented visually for quick scanning, while a structured and filterable table provides deeper control over the complete expense history.

Payroll Overview. A consolidated view of salary history and payslip detail. The net payable amount is pulled and visually emphasized as the primary figure, with the full deduction breakdown (mandatory, pension, optional) laid out beneath it for transparency. A year-over-year salary chart turns raw monthly figures into an at-a-glance trend, while the payslip table below supports filtering, PDF export, and quick access to underlying documents, turning a dense payroll report into a self-service financial summary.

Attendance Reports. A daily attendance log built for fast triage rather than raw data entry. Color-coded status chips at the top (Pending Approval, Comments, Anomalies, Missing Reports) act as one-click filters, letting managers jump straight to the records that need attention instead of scanning the full table. Flagged rows (e.g. a missing sick-note attachment) are highlighted inline with contextual actions (comment, attach a file, resolve) so exceptions can be handled without leaving the table. Editable approval statuses per row, bulk save/cancel controls, and an Excel export keep the workflow efficient for high-volume, day-to-day review.
